Short term absence

Triggered at 3 absences in a rolling 6-month period, or a noticeable pattern

1
First absence review meeting

Discuss reasons for absence, any underlying factors and what support may be needed. Outcome confirmed in writing within 14 days.

First absence warning — live 6 months
2
Second absence review meeting

Further absence while the first warning is active. Follow the same process. When considering the reasoning behind the absence, is Occupational Health an appropriate next step? Outcome confirmed in writing within 14 days.

Second absence warning — live 9 months
3
Third absence review meeting

Further absence while the second warning is active. Advise in writing that a possible outcome is a final warning or dismissal.

Final warning (12 months) or dismissal
Things to consider before delivering an outcome
  • Is there an underlying medical condition contributing to the absence?
  • Consider whether the Equality Act may apply
  • Have reasonable adjustments been discussed and explored?
  • Is there a pattern that could suggest a work-related cause?
  • When considering the reasoning behind the absence, is Occupational Health an appropriate next step?
  • Has the Employee Assistance Programme (EAP) been offered?
  • Has the colleague been given a genuine opportunity to improve?

Long term absence

Absences of 4 weeks or more — arrange a review and keep in regular contact throughout

1
Initial review at 4 weeks

Arrange a review meeting to discuss health, support available and reasonable adjustments. Consider an Occupational Health referral. Keep in regular contact throughout the absence.

Keep in regular contact throughout
2
Ongoing review meetings

No fixed timescales — arrange after OH reports or GP updates. At each meeting discuss health progress, adjustments, OH report findings and return to work plans.

3
Formal review meeting

If return within a reasonable timeframe looks unlikely, arrange a formal meeting. The outcome may be dismissal on the grounds of capability.

Confirm outcome in writing with appeal rights
Things to consider before moving to capability
  • When considering the reasoning behind the absence, is Occupational Health an appropriate next step?
  • Has the Employee Assistance Programme (EAP) been offered?
  • Consider whether the Equality Act may apply
  • Have all reasonable adjustments been fully explored?
  • Has a phased return to work been considered?
  • Has the colleague been kept regularly informed throughout?

Welfare, maternity & unauthorised absence

Keeping in contact, managing non-return and non-attendance

✓
Welfare checks

Agree contact frequency at the first call in. Contact the colleague the day before a fit note expires. Document all welfare contact made throughout the absence.

M
Maternity non-return

If a colleague does not return on their agreed date, contact them as soon as possible. Send the non-return letter and allow a reasonable period for a response.

!
Unauthorised absence

If no contact by the correct method, treat the absence as unauthorised. Attempt contact by phone, then try the emergency contact. Document all attempts and times.

May result in disciplinary action
