Disciplinary process

Key principle: A disciplinary hearing should only take place following a fact find / investigation where the evidence warrants formal action. The process must be fair, consistent and properly documented.

Step 1 Prepare and invite
  • Notify the colleague in writing of the allegations and the basis of those allegations
  • Include a copy of the disciplinary policy and full evidence pack
  • Confirm their right to be accompanied by a work colleague or trade union representative
  • Confirm a note taker will be present
Step 2 Disciplinary hearing
  • Chair must be a manager of appropriate seniority
  • Go through the allegations and all evidence gathered
  • Give the colleague full opportunity to respond and present their own evidence
  • Companion may make representations and ask questions but cannot answer on the colleague's behalf
  • Take detailed notes the hearing may be adjourned if further investigation is needed
  • Decision confirmed in writing usually within one week of the hearing
Step 3 Weigh up and decide
  • Review all evidence carefully after the hearing
  • Consider the colleague's response and any mitigating circumstances
  • Consider any previous disciplinary history
  • Decision must be made within 7 working days of the hearing
Step 4 Confirm in writing
  • Confirm decision and reasons in writing usually within one week
  • State the nature of the complaint and required standards
  • Include timescales for improvement where applicable
  • Place record on personnel file
  • Inform of right to appeal within 5 working days

Frequently asked questions
Who can act as a companion? +
A work colleague or a full-time official employed by a trade union. The companion can explain and sum up the colleague's case and respond to views expressed, but cannot answer questions on their behalf. You cannot refuse a reasonable request to be accompanied.
How long does a warning stay on record? +
Both a written warning and a final written warning normally cease to have effect after 12 months. After this they are spent and should not be taken into account in future proceedings, though a record may remain on the personnel file.
What if the colleague fails to attend the hearing? +
The colleague must make every effort to attend. Failure to attend without good reason may be treated as misconduct in itself. If they are persistently unable to attend, the organisation may make a decision based on the available evidence.
Can Stage 1 be skipped? +
Yes if the conduct is sufficiently serious, Stage 1 may be skipped and proceed straight to a final written warning at Stage 2. Ensure this is clearly documented.
