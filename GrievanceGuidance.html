Grievance process

Key principle: The purpose of the grievance process is to give colleagues a fair and timely way to raise concerns about their work or working environment. Always try to resolve things informally first — formal action should be a last resort.

Step 1
Informal resolution
  • Talk to the colleague promptly and privately
  • Handle confidentially and supportively
  • Document discussions and actions in writing
  • If unresolved, move to formal process
Step 2
Grievance raised
  • Acknowledge in writing immediately
  • Arrange meeting within 7 calendar days
  • Should normally be raised within 1 month of the issue
  • Confirm right to be accompanied by a colleague or trade union rep
Step 3
Grievance meeting
  • Give colleague full opportunity to explain their grievance
  • Take detailed notes throughout
  • Copy of notes given to colleague after the meeting
  • May be adjourned if further investigation is needed
Step 4
Investigation & outcome
  • Carry out any further investigation needed
  • Consider all evidence before deciding
  • Confirm outcome in writing as soon as possible
  • Explain reasons for the decision clearly
Possible outcomes
Fully upheld
  • Confirm in writing that the grievance is upheld
  • Set out clearly what action will be taken
  • Follow up to ensure agreed actions are implemented
  • Colleague retains right to appeal
Partially upheld
  • Confirm which elements are upheld and which are not
  • Explain clearly why each element has been decided as it has
  • Set out what action will be taken for upheld elements
  • Colleague retains right to appeal the full outcome
Not upheld
  • Confirm in writing that the grievance is not upheld
  • Explain clearly why there was insufficient evidence
  • Colleague retains right to appeal
  • Deliberately untrue allegations may be dealt with under the disciplinary process
Appeal process
1
Submit appeal in writing
Within 7 calendar days of receiving the outcome letter. Must clearly state the grounds for appeal. Longer may be given in exceptional circumstances.
2
Appeal meeting arranged
Heard by a different manager where possible. Colleague has the right to be accompanied. Rescheduled meetings within one week of the original.
3
Outcome confirmed in writing
Decision confirmed in writing. There is no further right of appeal. If a grievance raised after leaving Be Caring, it will be investigated but a meeting may not always be held.
Grievance upheld
The original decision will be overruled and the grievance outcome rescinded.
Grievance partially upheld
Some elements of the grievance are supported. A revised outcome is confirmed in writing.
Grievance not upheld
The original decision will be upheld and the grievance outcome confirmed. No further right of appeal.
⚠️
Grievance raised during a disciplinary process

If the grievance is unrelated to the disciplinary matter, the disciplinary process continues and another manager handles the grievance separately. If the two matters are connected, you may either deal with them together or pause the disciplinary while the grievance is resolved.
