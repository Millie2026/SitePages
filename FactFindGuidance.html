What is a fact find?

The first step in addressing concerns — an informal meeting to gather all relevant facts before any decision is made. It is not a disciplinary hearing.

Why does it matter?

Failure to follow up concerns could lead to evidence being destroyed and potential tribunal claims. Consistent process protects everyone involved.

When to use it

Potential disciplinary matter, conflict between colleagues, allegations of bullying or harassment, complaint from a service user, or a safeguarding concern.

Fact find / investigation process

Key principle: The purpose of a fact find is to discover all relevant facts in a fair, reasonable and objective manner. Minor issues do not always require a full investigation — but all fact finds must be documented in writing with expectations set going forward.

Before you go in

  • Write down the specific concern before speaking to anyone
  • Review relevant records — care notes, PASS, MAR charts, rotas, logs
  • Identify who needs to be interviewed and in what order
  • Confirm who will conduct it — anyone without prior involvement
  • Inform the individual informally and advise of right to a companion

During the meeting

  • Confirm at the start this is a fact find, not a disciplinary
  • Ask open questions — give them full opportunity to respond
  • Take detailed notes in their own words, not paraphrased
  • Do not share one person's account with another
  • Interview witnesses separately and keep notes for each
  • Summarise at the end and ask if anything has been missed
Questions to ask
Useful questions
Can you tell me in your own words what happened?
What happened next? Walk me through the sequence of events.
Who else was present? What did they say or do?
What impact do you think your actions had?
If you're saying that didn't happen — can you help me understand what did?
Is there anything else you think I should know before I make a decision?
What would you want to happen next, and why?
Is there anything going on outside of work that may be relevant?
⚖️

Consistent process. Not identical outcomes.

Two people can go through exactly the same process and reach different outcomes — and that is right. People are different, circumstances are different. What must be consistent is how you conduct the fact find and how you record your decision. The outcome is not black and white.

Factors that influence the outcome
📅

Length of service & history

A first concern in four years is very different to a repeated pattern of the same behaviour.

🤝

Acceptance of responsibility

Whether the individual acknowledges what happened and shows genuine insight matters when deciding next steps.

💛

Mitigating circumstances

Personal pressures, health, or staffing levels outside the individual's control may reduce the severity of the response.

🔁

Pattern or isolated incident

A single lapse in judgement is different to a pattern. Has a similar concern been raised before, even informally?

👥

Impact on others

The effect on service users, colleagues, or the organisation is a key factor in deciding whether informal resolution is appropriate.

📚

Training & awareness

Was the individual trained and aware of the relevant policy? Lack of awareness points toward support rather than sanction.
